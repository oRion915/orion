# Project Orion

## Mission

Project Orion exists to build software that people can rely on when accuracy, continuity, and accountability matter. We serve users by turning complexity into dependable tools and by treating their trust as a responsibility, not a metric.

## Vision

Orion aims to endure: technically, organizationally, and ethically. We build systems that remain understandable, useful, and worthy of confidence as requirements, teams, and technologies change.

## Engineering Philosophy

Engineering is the disciplined practice of making correct, comprehensible, and maintainable choices under real constraints. We favor clarity over cleverness, evidence over assumption, and durable simplicity over short-lived convenience.

## Core Principles

- Put user trust ahead of speed, novelty, and local optimization.
- Make behavior explicit, predictable, and observable.
- Prefer small, reversible changes that can be understood and verified.
- Treat correctness, security, privacy, and accessibility as shared responsibilities.
- Leave every part of the project clearer than it was found.
- Design for failure, recovery, and graceful degradation.

## The Trust Rule

Never knowingly trade a user’s trust for convenience. Be honest about uncertainty, preserve the integrity of user data, communicate limitations plainly, and correct mistakes promptly. A feature is not successful if it works while making the system less trustworthy.

## The 100-Year Rule

Make decisions as though someone will need to understand, operate, and extend Orion a century from now. We cannot predict the future, but we can choose durable names, clear boundaries, documented reasoning, and designs that welcome responsible change.

## The Ship of Orion Rule

Orion may evolve piece by piece without losing its identity. Replace components when doing so improves reliability, clarity, or safety, while preserving the principles and user commitments that define the project. Continuity comes from values and behavior, not from keeping every part unchanged.

## AI Philosophy

AI is a collaborator, not an authority. It can accelerate exploration and reduce routine effort, but people remain accountable for intent, judgment, verification, and consequences. AI-assisted work must meet the same standards of review, security, privacy, clarity, and correctness as all other work.

## Architecture Philosophy

Architecture should make the right thing easier to do and the unsafe thing harder to do. Favor explicit boundaries, limited coupling, coherent ownership, and designs that can be tested and changed with confidence. Introduce complexity only when it earns its cost through demonstrated value.

## Project Structure Philosophy

The project’s structure is part of its interface to contributors. Organize work so that purpose, ownership, dependencies, and change impact are easy to discover. Keep related concerns together, separate unrelated concerns, and avoid structures that require tribal knowledge to navigate.

## Contributor Principles

Contributors are stewards of a shared, long-lived system. Read before changing, seek context before optimizing, test claims before relying on them, and document decisions that are not self-evident. Welcome review as a means of improving the work, not merely approving it. Disagree respectfully, decide clearly, and support the decision once made.

## Decision Making Process

Start with the user need and the constraints that matter. Gather sufficient evidence, identify risks and tradeoffs, and choose the simplest option that satisfies the need without compromising Orion’s principles. Record consequential decisions in proportion to their impact. Revisit decisions when new evidence materially changes the situation.

## Long-Term Vision

Orion will become more capable without becoming less trustworthy. Its success will be measured by sustained user confidence, responsible stewardship, resilient engineering, and a contributor culture that improves the system for those who come next.
